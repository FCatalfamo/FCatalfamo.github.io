## Overview

This thesis examines the relationship between independent political expenditures and electoral outcomes in the 2020 U.S. House elections.

The analysis focuses on spending by outside groups, including Super PACs and other independent organisations that can raise and spend unlimited funds while remaining formally separate from candidates’ campaigns. It asks whether higher levels of independent expenditure are associated with better electoral performance.

## Research design

The study combines district-level electoral data with information on independent expenditures during the 2020 election cycle. It considers the methodological challenges involved in estimating the relationship between campaign spending and electoral outcomes, particularly the risk that spending responds to expected competitiveness rather than causing electoral success.

## Contribution

Rather than treating campaign finance only as a question of direct donations to candidates, the thesis examines the growing role of external actors in American elections after *Citizens United*.

It offers an empirical assessment of how independent spending relates to electoral competition in the U.S. House, while discussing the difficulties of drawing causal conclusions from observational campaign-finance data.
