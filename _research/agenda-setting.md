## Overview

This paper examines whether campaign contributions shape legislative behaviour not by changing how senators vote, but by influencing what they choose to speak about.

I combine U.S. Senate floor speeches from 2011 to 2024 with sector-level campaign-finance data from OpenSecrets. Using a dictionary-based measure of topic salience, the analysis tests whether contributions from a given economic sector are associated with greater attention to the corresponding policy domain in senators’ speeches.

## Findings

The results provide partial but meaningful evidence of sector-topic congruence. In several policy domains, senators who receive higher contributions from a sector devote more attention to that sector’s issues in subsequent floor speeches.

At the same time, ideological positioning remains an important predictor of agenda priorities. Campaign finance therefore appears to operate through targeted shifts in issue attention, layered on top of stable ideological differences in political communication.

The paper contributes to research on money in politics by treating legislative speech as a form of political effort and agenda-setting, rather than focusing exclusively on roll-call votes.
