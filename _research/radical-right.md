## Overview

This paper investigates whether the parliamentary presence of radical-right parties affects the ideological cohesion of governing coalitions in European parliamentary democracies.

Using cabinet-level data from the early 1990s to the early 2020s, it examines the ideological distance between parties in government while accounting for party-system fragmentation, cabinet characteristics, polarization, and country-level differences.

## Findings

The apparent positive relationship between radical-right parliamentary representation and coalition ideological diversity disappears once institutional and cabinet-level factors are taken into account. Instrumental-variable estimates instead suggest that radical-right presence may be associated with narrower governing coalitions.

The results indicate that government cohesion is shaped more strongly by coalition structure and institutional constraints than by the radical right's parliamentary presence alone.
