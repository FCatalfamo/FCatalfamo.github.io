## Overview

Coalition governments require political parties that previously competed against one another to cooperate in government. This project examines whether such elite cooperation also changes how citizens perceive the parties involved.

The study focuses on the affective consequences of coalition formation and asks whether voters become more favorable toward parties that begin governing together with their preferred party.

## Research Question

How does coalition formation affect citizens' evaluations of political parties?

More specifically, the project examines whether the beginning of coalition cooperation reduces affective distance between supporters of coalition partners.

## Data and Research Design

The analysis uses cross-national panel survey data covering multiple episodes of coalition formation.

By comparing changes in respondents' evaluations of new coalition partners with changes in their evaluations of other parties, the design exploits within-individual variation in partisan affect around government formation.

## Contribution

The project contributes to research on affective polarization by shifting attention from political competition to political cooperation.

While much of the literature examines how conflict between political elites can intensify partisan animosity, coalition formation provides an opportunity to study whether visible cooperation between parties can move citizens' attitudes in the opposite direction.
