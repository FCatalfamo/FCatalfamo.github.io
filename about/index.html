About

About me.

I am a political scientist with a background in political science, communication and data analysis. My work focuses on political behaviour, party politics and political communication, with a broader interest in understanding how political institutions and elite behaviour shape citizens' attitudes.

Academic Profile

I am a PhD researcher in Political Science at the Network for the Advancement of Social and Political Studies (NASP), jointly affiliated with the University of Milan and the University of Pavia.

My research interests lie at the intersection of political behaviour, party politics and political communication, with a particular focus on affective polarization, coalition politics and citizens' responses to elite behaviour.

Methodologically, I am primarily interested in quantitative and comparative approaches to political science, including panel data, causal inference and text-as-data methods.

Background

My academic path combines political science, communication and quantitative social science. Before beginning my PhD, I completed an M.A. in Political Science at the University of Mannheim.

Prior to Mannheim, I studied at the University of Milan, where I completed an M.A. in Public and Corporate Communication, following the Data Analytics for Politics, Society and Complex Organizations curriculum, and a B.A. in Political Science.

This interdisciplinary background has shaped my interest in the relationship between political behaviour, communication and quantitative empirical research.

Professional Experience

Alongside my academic training, I have worked in policy research and political communication. My professional experience has included quantitative research, dataset construction and analysis, policy reports, literature reviews and public-facing communication.
